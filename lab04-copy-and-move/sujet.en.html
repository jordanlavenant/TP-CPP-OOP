# Context In lab 3, the copy constructor and the copy assignment operator of `shape` were declared with `= delete`, and nothing replaced them. Here, a `drawing` holds any number of shapes of any kind. It holds them through pointers, because their kinds are known only at run time, and through **owning** pointers, because no other object destroys them. That choice alone prevents copying: a `std::unique_ptr` cannot be copied, so neither can a vector of them, nor a class that holds such a vector without defining its own copy constructor. By the end of the session, the program writes `build/mandala.svg`: a motif of nine shapes copied twenty-four times, each copy scaled by a factor that depends on its position and rotated by 15 degrees more than the previous one.
The picture is correct only if each copy **owns its own shapes**: the arms are drawn at several different scales, while the original motif, declared `const`, stays unchanged. Question 4.4 replaces the copy with a reference and shows what happens to the picture.
